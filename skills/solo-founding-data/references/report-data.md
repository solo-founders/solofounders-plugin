# The State of Solo Founding — Data

Source: *The State of Solo Founding: Solo Founder Data & Statistics*, Solo Founders, December 2025. solofounders.com/report. Underlying data from Carta's dataset ("tens of thousands of companies"; no exact sample size published). Period mostly 2019–H1 2025.

Always cite as: *State of Solo Founding (Solo Founders × Carta data, Dec 2025)*.

## Definitions (Carta taxonomy)
- **Pre-seed:** SAFEs or convertible notes before the first priced equity round.
- **Priced equity:** round that sets a valuation, identified by share class in the charter.
- **Lead founder:** in a co-founded company, the founder with the largest stake (usually CEO). Comparisons are solo founder vs. lead founder.

## Share of new companies founded solo
Share of U.S. startups on Carta founded by a solo founder:

| 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | H1 2025 |
|------|------|------|------|------|------|---------|
| 23.7% | 24.5% | 26.0% | 27.2% | 27.8% | 30.5% | 36.3% |

- H1 2025 is the first time solo-founded companies passed one-third of new startups.
- 2019 → H1 2025: +12.6 points (~13 points in about five years, per Peter Walker, Carta), a ~53% relative increase.
- Rose every year in the series; the biggest jump is 2024 → H1 2025 (+5.8 points).
- H1 2025 is a half-year figure; say so when quoting it.
- Investor estimate (not Carta data): solo-founder share of *cash raised* rose from ~10% (2019) to the mid-teens (Ann Miura-Ko, Floodgate).

## Sectors
- SaaS, Consumer, and Healthtech ≈ two-thirds of Carta startups for both groups.
- Consumer Products & Services: 17.0% of solo vs. 12.0% of multi-founder.
- Pharma & Biotech: 5.3% of solo vs. 8.6% of multi-founder.

## Demographics
- Women are a slightly larger share of solo founders than of multi-founder teams (no figure published).
- Same geographic hubs; Bay Area shows the widest gap (no figure published).

## Fundraising
- Solo-founded companies raise less in aggregate; both groups followed the same cycle (2021 peak, 2022–23 decline, 2024 partial rebound).
- Solo companies are **less likely** to raise pre-seed in year one.
- Solo companies raise their **first priced round sooner** than co-founded, in every cohort 2018–2024.
- From Priced Seed through Series B, valuations, dilution, and round sizes are **comparable**.
- 2024: Series A round sizes nearly identical; Priced Seed and Series B slightly lower for solo.
- Median Priced Seed valuation likely lower for solo (no figure published).

## Dilution
- 2024: early-stage dilution nearly identical for solo and co-founded companies.
- Peter Walker (Carta): solo founders who convince a VC face no dilution penalty.
- Anecdote: Jimmy Douglas (Plug) expected ~25% dilution vs. ~20% for a comparable co-founded company.

## Ownership and exits
- Solo founders hold substantially more than lead founders through early rounds.
- **By Series B: ~50% more personal ownership** for solo founders (comparable valuation and dilution assumed).
- **At exit (2019–H1 2025): median ownership 75% greater** for solo founders than lead founders.
- Caveat: higher ownership ≠ better outcome unless exit values are comparable; the data doesn't capture exit values.
- Time to exit: solo was slower in earlier cohorts; recently slightly faster.

## Team
- Solo companies make their **first hire earlier**. Both groups now wait longer than a few years ago.
- Engineering is the most common first hire for both.
- When the first hire isn't engineering: operations is 14.5% for solo vs. 10.7% for multi-founder.
- First-five-employee median equity grants (2023–24 hires): nearly identical across groups.
- Field notes (anecdotal, Julian Weisser): solo founders often grant 2–5x median equity to early employees; consider 6-year vesting for large grants with the standard 1-year cliff.

## AI
- No quantified AI metrics. Cited qualitatively: more individual leverage, lower startup costs, faster prototyping, fewer co-founder needs, possibly faster pre-seed for AI companies.

## Related figures from the podcast (not the report)
- 25–30% of Precursor portfolio companies lose a co-founder before Series A (Charles Hudson, Ep. 3).
- ~65% of high-potential startup failures trace to co-founder conflict (Noam Wasserman, *The Founder's Dilemmas*; cited Ep. 28).
