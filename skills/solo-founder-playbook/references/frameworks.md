# Recurring Frameworks

Grouped by the problem a founder brings. Episode numbers point to `episodes.md`.

## 1. The co-founder decision

- **Denominator delusion.** "Most successful startups have co-founders" ignores how many co-founded startups failed. Base rates, not survivors. (Julian Weisser, Ep. 8, 28; Charles Hudson, Ep. 3)
- **Co-founder of convenience.** Taking whoever is available because investors expect a team. Worse than solo. (Ep. 8, 14, 28)
- **Prerequisite trap.** Treating a co-founder as a prerequisite turns the search into a substitute for building. Searching is how you get a bad one. (Ep. 26; Rahul Sonwalkar, Ep. 7)
- **Breakup math.** 25–30% of Precursor portfolio companies lose a co-founder before Series A (Hudson, Ep. 3). ~65% of high-potential failures trace to co-founder conflict (Wasserman, cited Ep. 28). Dead equity follows.
- **Blood bond myth.** Ask who will actually stay for ten years. (Michael Grinich, Ep. 13)
- **One real decider.** Most teams have one decision-maker anyway. (Eugenia Kuyda, Ep. 4) Debate without decision is worse than a wrong call. (Ben Cera, Ep. 38)
- **Find one by building.** Elevate a great early hire later; evaluate late co-founders like senior hires. (Ep. 26; Hudson, Ep. 3)
- **Taxonomy.** True Solo (venture-backed, building a team) / Free Solo (no investors, no search) / Juiced Solo (AI-amplified one-person output). (Ep. 8, 38)
- **Bear case.** First-time founders lack the five tools — product, sales, fundraising, hiring, ops (Paul Klein IV, Ep. 2). Loneliness is the real failure mode (Ep. 3, 28).

## 2. Picking the idea

- **Original insight.** No non-consensus insight into the industry → don't start. (Sunil Rajaraman, Ep. 9)
- **Right to win.** Which idea deserves your resignation letter? (John Glasgow, Ep. 25)
- **Four-question constraint filter.** One person can build it; under ~$40K with no raise; you're the domain expert; you can empathize with the buyer. Constraints rank options. (David Phillips, Ep. 11)
- **Ten-year / market-personality fit.** Would you work on this for a decade? (Thais Castello Branco, Ep. 18; Sara Du, Ep. 32)
- **Venture-sized and fast.** Big isn't enough; the market must move fast enough. (Jimmy Douglas, Ep. 10)
- **Extension of you.** Build what extends your history; mine old customer notes. (Yakko Majuri, Ep. 24) The most personal is the most universal. (Ep. 14)
- **10,000-dinners test.** Would you talk about this at 10,000 dinners? (Dave Morin, Ep. 30)
- **Memo validation.** Write the ~3,000-word memo; if it doesn't hold up on paper, it won't in market. (Klein, Ep. 2)
- **Don't solve your own problem by default.** (Minn Kim, Ep. 12)
- **Disprove, don't prove.** Design tests that could kill the hypothesis. (Douglas, Ep. 10)

## 3. Fundraising alone

- **Don't apologize for being solo.** (Hudson, Ep. 3)
- **"We don't back solo founders"** is often an easy out for a pass; investors read your bar through your hires. (Weisser, Ep. 28)
- **No optics co-founder.** Don't hand out 40% to look like a team. (Hudson, Ep. 3)
- **Angels.** $1K–$5K checks from great people start rounds; more angels > fewer; customers (yours and competitors') make great angels; never take money for a promised intro. (Weisser, Ep. 37)
- **Raise from traction.** Declined VCs for nine months (Dhravya Shah, Ep. 15). "The best round is when you're not raising." (Zach Laberge, Ep. 27)
- **Relationships over process.** (Klein, Ep. 2)
- **Pre-empt objections.** Write down every objection before each meeting. (Douglas, Ep. 10)
- **Optimize investor quality over valuation.** (Ep. 28)
- **Free solo is a real option.** $9M ARR, zero investors (Yasser Elsaid, Ep. 6); ChatPRD bootstrapped (Claire Vo, Ep. 20). Venture scale is a choice and a commitment (Ep. 37).
- **Non-dilutive start.** Microgrants $100–$1,000 (Ari Dutilh, Ep. 19).

## 4. First hires and team

- **Contract-to-hire / work trials.** (Klein, Ep. 2; Francis, Ep. 5; Kim, Ep. 12; Du, Ep. 32)
- **Multipliers, not delegators.** Prefer candidates who want more equity, less cash. (Sonwalkar, Ep. 7)
- **Be generous with early equity.** (Daniel Francis, Ep. 5; Chai Choudhary, Ep. 36) Field notes: solo founders often grant 2–5x median to early employees (State of Solo Founding report).
- **Founding team, not co-founders.** Seven-person founding team with real ownership (Francis Davidson, Ep. 16); reset the team at the seed (Laberge, Ep. 27); build the founding team instead of hunting (Ali Ansari, Ep. 33).
- **Chief-of-staff trap.** Hire operations; keep building yourself. (Paul Bakaus, Ep. 23)
- **Engineers first** — even in a services business. (Kim, Ep. 12)
- **Attract, don't chase; minimal headcount; no cold hires.** (Du, Ep. 32; Shivani Poddar, Ep. 34)
- **Hire true builders from open source.** (Shah, Ep. 15)
- **Agents are management.** Scope AI agents like employees with explicit authority. (Vo, Ep. 20; Cera, Ep. 1)
- **Brain trust / speed-dial bench / advisors.** Covers what you're not. Weekly 1:1s and templated advisor equity (FAST). (Flo Crivello, Ep. 17; Poddar, Ep. 34; Laberge, Ep. 27; Rajaraman, Ep. 9)

## 5. Sales and distribution

- **Founder-led sales; never hand it off early.** (Grinich, Ep. 13; Sonwalkar, Ep. 7)
- **Enterprise from the buyer's side.** Champions, budget cycles, price under approval thresholds. (Glasgow, Ep. 25)
- **Calendar spearing.** Compress every step to "yes." (Ep. 29)
- **Content and free-first.** (Elsaid, Ep. 6; Bakaus, Ep. 23; Choudhary, Ep. 36; Shah, Ep. 15)
- **Pricing is the fastest lever; founders underprice.** (Elsaid, Ep. 6; Rajaraman, Ep. 9)
- **Every setup step loses users.** (Cera, Ep. 38)
- **Retention is growth.** Smile curve vs. leaky bucket; hype vs. paying customers. (Ep. 26)
- **Be the only, not the best.** (Ep. 29)
- **Services scale like software now.** (Kim, Ep. 12; Ep. 29)

## 6. Operating alone

- **Momentum cures burnout, not rest.** (Crivello, Ep. 17, 35)
- **Front-load the work you avoid.** (Vo, Ep. 20, 35)
- **Decide.** Two-way doors fast, one-way doors slow (Elsaid, Ep. 6). Let hard calls simmer (Rajaraman, Ep. 9). Not deciding is worse than wrong (Cera, Ep. 38).
- **Fake board meeting.** Force the hard question on a schedule. (Douglas, Ep. 10)
- **Cadence.** Daily / weekly / monthly / quarterly. (Klein, Ep. 2) Async: open threads, final actions, no meetings. (Ansari, Ep. 33)
- **Don't defer life.** Deferring life causes burnout. (Glasgow, Ep. 25) Build a business that serves your life. (Vo, Ep. 20)
- **Solo, together.** Community replaces what a co-founder provides emotionally. (Ep. 8, 26, 28)
- **Prospect, don't just operate.** (Crivello, Ep. 17)
- **Hope runs out before money.** (Weisser, Ep. 8)

## 7. Pivot, kill, or restart

- **Shutdown test; wind down well.** (Laberge, Ep. 27)
- **Three signals to move on; only founders can inject risk.** Deleted $7M run rate. (Ansari, Ep. 33)
- **When to kill, how deep to cut.** (Crivello, Ep. 17)
- **Kill ideas fast; never marry a solution.** (Choudhary, Ep. 36)
- **False bottom.** First customer, then three dead months — normal. (Phillips, Ep. 11)
- **Pivots are expensive.** (Grinich, Ep. 13) But 14 GTM pivots got Plug there (Douglas, Ep. 10). Pivot GTM, not mission.
- **Customer-led pivots.** (Laberge, Ep. 27)
- **Inventor's dilemma.** Be willing to kill your own viral hit. (Shah, Ep. 15)
