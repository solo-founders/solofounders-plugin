# Solo Founders Podcast — Episode Index

Host: Julian Weisser (founder of Solo Founders; previously co-founder of On Deck/ODF). Full episodes: solofounders.com/podcast and youtube.com/@solofounding.

Cite as: **Guest (Company), Solo Founders Podcast, Ep. N**. Paraphrase. Never invent a lesson that is not listed here; if the founder needs more, send them to the episode.

Numbers are as stated by guests or show notes at time of recording. Company claims are marked.

---

## Ep. 1 — Ben Cera, Polsia (Mar 2026)
AI company builder. Ex–Global GM at CloudKitchens.
- $1.5M ARR in two weeks; ~1,500 companies running on the platform at recording.
- 80/20 framework for AI-native companies: ~80% of work done by AI, ~20% is human taste and direction.
- Scope AI agents like employees: trust boundaries, explicit authority.
- Build for yourself, not imaginary customers.
- Push AI to the edge of what it can do; run the "100% autonomous company" thought experiment.

## Ep. 2 — Paul Klein IV, Browserbase (Mar 2026)
Headless browser infra for AI agents. ~$300M valuation in under 14 months. Applied to 500 internships, rejected by 498.
- Contrarian view: first-time founders should probably not go solo.
- The five-tool founder: product, sales, fundraising, hiring, operations.
- Validated the idea with a ~3,000-word memo.
- Solo by circumstance: tried and failed to find a co-founder.
- Hire contractors as paid work trials; recruit via Twitter DMs.
- Fundraising is relationship building, not a tight process.
- CEO arc: quarterback → head coach → general manager.
- Cadence: daily standups, weekly syncs, monthly all-hands, quarterly board.

## Ep. 3 — Charles Hudson, Precursor Ventures (Mar 2026)
Solo GP, 500+ investments.
- 25–30% of his portfolio companies lose a co-founder before Series A.
- Dead equity from departed co-founders damages cap tables.
- The denominator delusion: failed co-founding teams are never counted.
- Do not hand someone 40% of the company for fundraising optics.
- Do not apologize for being solo when raising.
- Evaluate late-joining co-founders like senior hires with unusual equity.
- Loneliness is far worse than founders expect ("10x").

## Ep. 4 — Eugenia Kuyda, Replika / Wabi (Mar 2026)
Replika reached 30M users.
- Most co-founder teams have one real decision-maker anyway.
- Default co-founders create misalignment at the top; solo beats misaligned.
- Cut company layers from three to two.
- Storytelling is a founder advantage.

## Ep. 5 — Daniel Francis, Abel Police (Apr 2026)
AI drafts police reports from body-cam footage. Self-taught engineer, high school dropout. Did 39 ride-alongs.
- Officers spend about a third of shifts on reports.
- Missionary over mercenary founding.
- Solo authorship produces product coherence.
- Write culture norms into a handbook early.
- Give early hires more equity; don't be greedy.
- Use contracting as the hiring pipeline.

## Ep. 6 — Yasser Elsaid, Chatbase (Apr 2026)
AI customer service. $9M ARR bootstrapped over three years, zero investors, ~30 people.
- Built v1 in six weeks; first Stripe payment 30 minutes after pricing page went live.
- In a paradigm shift, first principles beat market research.
- "Free solo": no investors, no co-founder search — and still not a lifestyle business.
- Solo founders decide faster. Change your mind with low ego.
- Two-way doors: decide fast. One-way doors: slow down.
- Get profitable, then spend. Pricing is the fastest lever. Margins matter less early.
- Self-serve plus a sales layer, on top of content.

## Ep. 7 — Rahul Sonwalkar, Julius AI (Apr 2026)
Natural-language data analysis. Ex-Uber. Six pivots, including a Microsoft cease-and-desist over an "Excel Copilot" name.
- Searching for a co-founder becomes a substitute for building.
- Startups depend on outliers; generic advice pulls you toward average.
- Hire multipliers, not delegators.
- Less cash, more equity is a positive signal in a candidate.
- Early hires set the culture.
- Sales is the real founder job.
- Solo means more persuading, not less: authority does not make people follow you.

## Ep. 8 — Julian Weisser, Solo Founders (interviewed by David Phillips) (Apr 2026)
- Over 1 in 3 new companies are now solo-founded, up from under 25% five years earlier (Carta).
- Denominator delusion; co-founder-of-convenience trap.
- Taxonomy: **True Solo** (solo, venture-backed, building a team), **Free Solo** (no investors, no co-founder search), **Juiced Solo** (one person amplified by AI to company-scale output).
- Companies run out of hope before they run out of money.
- "Solo alone" vs. "solo together": community replaces what a co-founder functionally provides.
- Startups as art; against "contortionism" — bending yourself to fit investor templates.

## Ep. 9 — Sunil Rajaraman, Hamlet (Apr 2026)
Civic AI for local government. Ex-Scripted co-founder, ex-GoodRx.
- ~$2T of GDP flows through local government; most council votes are decided before the meeting.
- A resident newsletter became the company.
- Original-insight test: if you don't have a non-consensus insight into the industry, don't start.
- Niche obsession is the key test.
- Editorial voice can't be copied.
- Front-load advisor relationships as a co-founder substitute.
- Pricing humility: founders underprice.
- Let hard decisions simmer before committing.

## Ep. 10 — Jimmy Douglas, Plug (May 2026)
EV wholesale marketplace. Ex-Tesla. $6.7M seed (Floodgate), $20M Series A (Lightspeed) during an EV downturn.
- 14 go-to-market pivots.
- Try to disprove your hypothesis, not prove it (credits Mike Maples Jr.).
- Ran a fake board meeting to force the hard question.
- Dropped the pure-marketplace model, got a dealer license, bought inventory: unique sellers per quarter 16 → 429.
- Test whether a market is both venture-sized *and* moving fast enough.
- Anticipate investor objections before every meeting.
- Self-imposed constraints as a tactic. Recruit high-agency people out of big companies.
- Respect investor pass etiquette.

## Ep. 11 — David Phillips, Fondo (May 2026)
Accounting/tax for venture-backed startups. Four co-founder breakups across earlier startups.
- Refounded alone with $40K and one investor email.
- Four-question idea filter: can one person build it; under $40K with no fundraise; am I the domain expert; can I empathize with the customer.
- Co-founder breakups: handle severance and stock cleanly, then move on.
- The "false bottom": first customer, then three months of nothing.
- A Delaware franchise-tax mini-product brought the first ten paying customers.
- AI tools substitute for much of what a co-founder provided.

## Ep. 12 — Minn Kim, Lighthouse (May 2026)
AI-powered immigration firm for frontier-tech companies. Not a lawyer; first two hires were engineers.
- Services now scale like software: break the expertise in people's heads into components. Customers buy the result.
- "Solve your own problem" is not always right.
- Complicated vs. complex: a framework for founder fit and capital structure.
- Convert contractors to full-time.
- A 30-question anonymous form to surface blind spots.

## Ep. 13 — Michael Grinich, WorkOS (May 2026)
Enterprise-readiness infra. ~$2B company.
- The co-founder "blood bond" is a myth; ask who will actually stay for ten years.
- The founder "mental disorder" — one person with it is enough.
- Pick ideas with a notebook and four filters; borrows lessons from stand-up comedy (test material constantly).
- Pivots are expensive; avoid needing them.
- Founder-led sales is the purest test of value. Never hand off sales early; hire a head of sales as a partner later.

## Ep. 14 — Five founders, zero co-founders (compilation) (Jun 2026)
Ben Cera, Yasser Elsaid, Paul Klein IV, Eugenia Kuyda, Daniel Francis, Charles Hudson.
- A talented solo founder beats a mismatched team.
- Taste, judgment, and direction are the core of the job.
- One voice, one layer of alignment.
- The most personal work is often the most universal.
- Mission is a forcing function for decisions.

## Ep. 15 — Dhravya Shah, Supermemory (Jun 2026)
Open-source memory layer for AI agents. Sold first company at 16; raised $3M at 19. 26K+ GitHub stars.
- Raise as a result of traction; he declined VCs for nine months.
- Build "art" in public until it becomes a company.
- The inventor's dilemma: be willing to kill your own viral hits.
- Went solo after a co-founder breakup ended an earlier company.
- Beware Goodhart's Law on benchmarks; build evals that matter.
- Hire "true builders" from open source.

## Ep. 16 — Francis Davidson, Odessia (Jun 2026)
AI travel agent. Previously founded Sonder (public, multi-billion valuation, then collapse).
- Went solo on the comeback despite having had strong co-founders.
- Coding agents make skipping the MVP viable in some cases.
- Seven-person founding team with real ownership instead of co-founders.
- Founder mode vs. hiring executives too early.

## Ep. 17 — Flo Crivello, Lindy (Jun 2026)
AI agents. ~$50M raised solo. Rebuilt after a market-driven shutdown.
- Going solo should be the rule, not the exception.
- When to kill a company, and how deep to cut.
- "The cure to burnout is not rest. It's momentum."
- Prospecting for gold vs. operating the mine: founders belong in prospecting.
- Build a brain trust instead of a co-founder.

## Ep. 18 — Thais Castello Branco, Taste Labs (Jul 2026)
"Taste layer for AI." Ex-Exa. $18.5M raised.
- Taste is a measurable quality bar.
- AI slop comes from volume, not weak models.
- Most people are bad at commissioning AI work.
- Built an ~800-person tastemaker community.
- Ten-year test: pick a problem worth a decade.

## Ep. 19 — Ari Dutilh, Solo Grants (Jul 2026)
- Microgrants of $100–$1,000, no equity, no payback; it doesn't need to be a business (sologrants.com).
- Lineage: 1517 Fund's $1,000 checks, Emergent Ventures, Thiel Fellowship.
- First-time builders rarely look for co-founders at all.
- Funders often say "be more ambitious" — ask for what the project actually needs.

## Ep. 20 — Claire Vo, ChatPRD (Jul 2026)
AI for product managers. 100K+ PMs served. Bootstrapped, zero VC. Runs with ~nine AI agents. Ex-CTO Color, ex-CPTO LaunchDarkly.
- "Agents are just management": scope agents like employees.
- The model is not the moat.
- The "20-year overnight success": earned expertise is the advantage.
- Authorship over ownership: build a business that serves your life.
- Front-load the work you avoid.

## Ep. 22 — 6 VC-backed solo founders (compilation) (Jul 2026)
Rahul Sonwalkar, Sunil Rajaraman, Jimmy Douglas, David Phillips, Minn Kim, Michael Grinich. Summaries under their own episodes.

## Ep. 23 — Paul Bakaus, Impeccable (Jul 2026)
Creator of jQuery UI, ex-Google, sold a startup to Zynga. Backed by a16z.
- First hire was a chief of staff — and it was a trap: hire operations, keep building yourself.
- The era of the 40-year-old founder: intuition and craft as leverage.
- Free-first distribution: no ads, no outbound, only posts.
- Judgment over generation; the founder as editor.

## Ep. 24 — Yakko Majuri, Railcode (Aug 2026)
Early PostHog engineer (6th employee).
- Handing out Claude Code isn't an AI strategy.
- One-question test: are you holding your team back?
- Most software is going internal.
- Find your idea by pulling the thread through old customer notes.
- Build the thing that's an extension of you.

## Ep. 25 — John Glasgow, Campfire (Aug 2026)
AI-native ERP. $100M+ raised. Non-technical; 15 years in corporate finance; founded at 36 with kids; ~two years pre-PMF.
- "Right to win" filter: which ideas deserve your resignation letter.
- Enterprise sales from the buyer's side: champions, budget cycles, pricing under a buyer's approval threshold (his example: ~$95K).
- Deferring life causes burnout.
- The technical co-founder requirement is over in the AI era.

## Ep. 26 — 4 VC-backed solo founders (Aug 2026)
Aldo Del Valle, Chai Choudhary, Namanyay Goel, Dhravya Shah.
- The prerequisite trap: searching for a co-founder is how you get a bad one.
- Failure modes: family, close friends, misaligned incentives.
- Find a co-founder by building — elevate a first hire.
- Retention is growth: smile curve vs. leaky bucket.
- Hype vs. paying customers: the 10x question.

## Ep. 27 — Zach Laberge, Omen (Aug 2026)
Fluid intelligence for AI data centers. $41.5M raised; no degree; age 20.
- The shutdown test: know when to stop; wind down well.
- Restart from strengths: raised $3M in three days from buyers who had said no.
- Customer-led pivots: engines → hydraulics → data-center coolant.
- Reset the team at the seed; build a founding team without co-founders.
- "The best round is when you're not raising."
- Advisors: weekly 1:1s, templated equity (Founder Institute FAST agreement, fi.co/fast).

## Ep. 28 — Julian Weisser: "We don't back solo founders" (Sep 2026)
- ~65% of high-potential startup failures trace to co-founder conflict (Noam Wasserman, *The Founder's Dilemmas*).
- Raise the bar; never settle for a co-founder of convenience.
- Loneliness is the real failure mode; "solo, together" is the fix.
- Famous solo-founded companies: eBay, Dell, Zoom, Browserbase, WorkOS, Polsia.
- An investor's "solo" objection is often an easy out for a pass.
- Investors read your talent bar through your hires.
- The dilution advantage: optimize for investor quality over valuation.
- "Solo founder syndrome," debunked.

## Ep. 29 — Office Hours: 5 founders' hardest problems (Sep 2026)
- Monetize open source by charging for core value, not perks.
- "I want to sell a product" can be the mistake; services as software.
- Calendar spearing: compress every step between "I have this problem" and "yes."
- Customers as the orienting force; building is the addictive default.
- "Don't be the best, be the only."
- Feature requests are jobs to be done; the micro-app middle path.
- Hardware: ship the mind before the body.

## Ep. 30 — Dave Morin, Offline Ventures (Sep 2026)
Early Facebook; founded Path; co-founded Slow Ventures.
- AI tools and YouTube can do much of a co-founder's job.
- Startups die on relationships; investors as producers.
- The 10,000-dinners test for what to build.

## Ep. 31 — James Clift, Durable (Sep 2026)
AI website builder. 1M users in three months; ~3.5M sign-ups; $14M Series A (Spark). Sold VisualCV in 2020.
- Mission: make owning a business easier than having a job.
- Door-to-door sales lessons apply to fundraising.
- Make a dollar on your own at least once.

## Ep. 32 — Sara Du, Ando (Sep 2026)
$20M seed (Accel, Index, Emergence). Previously co-founded Alloy at 19; Thiel Fellow.
- Market-personality fit: pick a problem worth ten years.
- Solo hiring playbook: attract, don't chase; contract-to-hire; minimalist headcount.
- Co-founder the first time, solo the second.

## Ep. 33 — Ali Ansari, micro1 (Oct 2026)
AI training data. $4B company at 25.
- Three signals it's time to move on from a business.
- Deleted a business with $7M run rate; only founders can inject risk.
- Solo by default: build a founding team instead of hunting for a co-founder.
- Async culture: open threads, final actions, no meetings.

## Ep. 34 — Shivani Poddar, Wajo (Oct 2026)
Ex-DeepMind, Meta, Alexa.
- AI agents break at the last mile.
- Seed the product with real users outside the Valley.
- No cold hires; a three-word recruiting message.
- The speed-dial bench: a roster of experts who cover what you're not.

## Ep. 35 — The cure to burnout isn't rest (compilation) (Oct 2026)
Dhravya Shah, Francis Davidson, Thais Castello Branco, Flo Crivello, Claire Vo, Paul Bakaus. See their episodes.

## Ep. 36 — Chai Choudhary, Workers (Oct 2026)
- Proof that software works is becoming the product.
- Timing; the cost of selling too early.
- Runway as a decision tool.
- Kill ideas fast; never marry a solution.
- Content-led distribution for deeply technical products.
- Use equity to land exceptional hires.

## Ep. 37 — Julian Weisser: angels for solo founders (Nov 2026)
- $1K–$5K checks from great people start rounds.
- Never take angel money for a promised intro; make yourself easy to forward.
- Angels are a fan base: more is better than fewer.
- Angels and lead VCs want different things.
- Venture scale is a choice and a commitment.
- Your customers — and your competitor's — make great angels.

## Ep. 38 — Ben Cera returns, Polsia (Nov 2026)
~$20M run rate about seven months after Ep. 1; raised $30M at a $250M valuation.
- Not deciding is worse than a wrong decision.
- Every setup step loses users.
- Founders as creative directors.
- True, free, and juiced solo, revisited.
