# Fundraise Solo

Help a solo founder raise without apologizing for being solo. Pick the mode from the request; if unclear, ask which one.

Load `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/solo-founder-playbook/references/frameworks.md` (section 3) and, for data claims, `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/solo-founding-data/references/report-data.md`.

## First: should they raise at all?

Ask once if not stated: traction, runway, what the money unlocks. If they have revenue and runway, raise the option of not raising now — raise from traction (Dhravya Shah, Ep. 15); "the best round is when you're not raising" (Zach Laberge, Ep. 27); Free Solo is viable (Yasser Elsaid, $9M ARR, zero investors, Ep. 6). Venture scale is a choice and a commitment (Ep. 37). Then proceed if they still want to raise.

## Mode A — Round strategy

- Round size tied to specific milestones, not a round number.
- **Angels first.** $1K–$5K checks from great people start rounds; more angels are better than fewer (Ep. 37). Prioritize: customers, competitors' customers, operators in the domain, other solo founders.
- Never accept money in exchange for a promised intro; make yourself easy to forward instead (Ep. 37).
- Optimize investor quality over valuation (Ep. 28).
- Relationship building, not a tight process (Paul Klein IV, Ep. 2).
- Never add an optics co-founder (Charles Hudson, Ep. 3).

If ~~CRM is connected, pull existing contacts and tag likely angels. Otherwise produce a target list template: name, why them, warm path, check size, status.

## Mode B — "We don't back solo founders"

Prepare the founder's answer. Structure:
1. **Don't apologize** (Hudson, Ep. 3). State it as a choice.
2. **Show the team bar** — investors read your talent through your hires and advisors (Ep. 28). Name the founding team, contractors, advisors, agents.
3. **Data, sparingly:** solo founders hold ~50% more ownership by Series B and dilution is comparable at early stages (State of Solo Founding, Dec 2025); 25–30% of co-founded teams lose a co-founder before Series A (Hudson, Ep. 3).
4. **Recognize the easy out.** If the objection is a polite pass, don't argue; ask what would change their mind and move on (Ep. 28).

Write a 3–4 sentence spoken answer in the founder's voice.

## Mode C — Deck or memo review

Read the attached deck/memo. Return:
- The one-sentence pitch as an investor would repeat it. If you can't produce one, that's finding #1.
- Top 5 objections an investor will raise, ranked, each with the slide that should answer it (Jimmy Douglas anticipates every objection before meetings, Ep. 10).
- Does the team slide make solo read as strength? Fix it if not.
- Cut list: slides to delete.

## Mode D — Meeting prep

Given an investor name (research them with web search): thesis, recent investments, solo-founder portfolio companies, likely objections, three questions to ask them, the ask.

## Mode E — Follow-ups and passes

Draft in ~~email if connected (as drafts, never send). Monthly update to prospects: one metric, one win, one ask. For a pass: thank, ask for the real reason and one intro-worthy fact, keep them on the update list. Respect pass etiquette (Douglas, Ep. 10).

## Output rules

Short, specific, in the founder's voice for anything they'll say or send. Cite episodes on strategic claims. Never fabricate investor facts — search or say unknown.
